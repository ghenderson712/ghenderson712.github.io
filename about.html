The idea

We share a country.
We can share some care.

America is an imperfect union of people whose lives are larger than the labels we give them. Kin Vow begins with a promise to recognize that humanity and act on it.

Taking a vow
to be kin.

Recognize each person's worth. Take responsibility for how you treat them.

A national hope, practiced close to home.

The founding hope is to help Americans treat one another with respect again. We can hold different beliefs and still take responsibility for the country we live in, beginning with our own conduct.

The invitation is ordinary: listen, keep your word, offer useful help when it is wanted, and acknowledge the harm you cause. A useful practice should fit into real life and leave room for mistakes, limits, and honest repair.

What kinship asks, and what it leaves to you.

Here, being kin means recognizing shared human worth. You choose your relationships. No one gains a claim to your time, loyalty, private experiences, trust, or forgiveness.

American civic life is the initial focus. Human worth does not depend on citizenship, ancestry, religion, or party membership.

Strong disagreement belongs here.

People can name injustice, criticize a policy, challenge a false claim, and set a firm boundary. Respect does not require silence about harm. Anger or discomfort alone is not misconduct.

The proposed movement's name, resources, and gatherings would not endorse political parties or candidates. Participants keep their own civic commitments.

Where we are

An idea ready
to be tested.

Kin Vow is a project in development. The vow, practice, and operating materials are drafts for a proposed adult pilot. No gathering, host organization, or official chapter is confirmed.

The proposed pilot would ask whether people return, find the practice useful, feel free to disagree, and can sustain the work. A decision to revise or stop would also be useful learning.

National growth would depend on responsible local hosting and experiences that others can repeat. We have no pilot results or evidence of national impact to report.

Kin Vow remains a working name pending rights review. No incorporated or tax-exempt organization is claimed here.

A question or an offer?

Email hello@kinvow.org for general questions about the developing project, the proposed adult pilot, or possible hosting or venue help.

No gatherings are scheduled. Emailing does not register you for an event or add you to a mailing list.

This mailbox is not continuously monitored. Do not email emergency requests, sensitive personal stories, details about minors, incident allegations, or raw evaluation responses. An independent human route for pilot complaints has not been arranged; a pilot cannot open until that route is usable.

Messages reach the project organizer through the email service. General inquiries may receive AI-assisted administrative help, such as sorting routine questions or drafting replies. Share only what is needed and leave out identifying details about other people.

Your practice can stay yours.

This site has no account, signup, tracking, or pledge registry. The reminder tool keeps your entries in the current page. It does not send them to Kin Vow or save them in site storage.

Copying uses your device's clipboard. Downloading creates a text file on your device. Printing opens your browser's print options. Those actions happen only when you choose them.

Reloading or leaving the page can discard your entries. Your browser or device may have its own saving features. Keep names and identifying details out of reminders.

If you choose to contact the project by email, the organizer receives your email address and message through the email service. General inquiries may receive AI-assisted administrative help. Sensitive stories, details about minors, incident allegations, and raw evaluation responses are excluded from that review. This is separate from the reminder tool. There is no automatic enrollment, and event registration and feedback would need their own information-handling details before a gathering opens.
