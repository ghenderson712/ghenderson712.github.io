About Kin Vow

Built by someone who lives the gap.

Kin Vow was not born in a boardroom. It was born from a teacher who understands firsthand what it feels like to know more than you can say.

The Founder

Gabriel Henderson

Gabriel is a teacher at River Valley High School. He works with students every day, including English Learners and refugees who carry entire worlds of knowledge inside them but struggle to express it in a system that was not designed for them.

But Gabriel does not approach this as an outsider looking in. He is dyslexic. He struggles with pronunciation, spelling, and real-time verbalization. He often feels trapped inside his own mind, knowing exactly what he means but fighting to connect those thoughts to the outside world.

That personal experience is the engine behind Kin Vow. When Gabriel sits with a student who cannot find the right English word, he does not see a language deficit. He sees himself. He sees the same gap he carries every day. And he thought: what if we stopped pretending this is some people's problem and started recognizing it as everyone's reality?

The Origin

How this started

The idea started simple: what if the "language barrier" is not just an immigrant experience or a disability label? What if it is the most universal thing about being human? Every person speaks two languages. The one inside them and the one that comes out. Those two are never the same.

That insight led to research. Polanyi's Paradox, Vygotsky's inner speech, the receptive-expressive gap, Bhabha's Third Space, Levelt's production model, Milton's Double Empathy Problem. Each one confirmed the same truth from a different angle: the gap between what you know and what you can express is built into how human cognition works.

The synthesis of these frameworks into a single community-building concept is Gabriel's original contribution. No existing organization, program, or movement was doing what Kin Vow set out to do: reframe the language barrier as shared human ground and build belonging from that foundation.

The Name

Why "Kin Vow"

"Kin" is Old English for your people. Not by blood. By choice. It comes from the Proto-Germanic kunja, the same root that gives us "kind" and "kin." Your kin are the ones you choose to stand with.

"Vow" is the solemn promise you make out loud. From the Latin votum, from the Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to speak solemnly." A vow is not casual. It is the kind of commitment you make when you mean it.

Together, Kin Vow is the commitment your chosen family makes: to hear each other, to hold space for the gap, and to believe that every person is worth listening to. Even when the words are not perfect. Especially then.

The acronym K.I.N. V.O.W. maps to the six pillars: Knowledge, Identity, Narrative, Voice, Oneness, Worth. Every letter carries weight.

Our Philosophy

"All I know is I know nothing, but I think a lot."

That is Gabriel's line, and it echoes the Socratic humility that runs through everything Kin Vow does. This organization does not claim to have answers. It claims to think deeply and learn together.

Truth here is dynamic. It evolves as the community grows, learns, and expands. What is true today gets refined tomorrow. Every understanding is a snapshot. The mutual understanding is that the dynamic nature of reality means this will grow into something more, something better, as the community expands.

"This project is itself a living example of its own mission."

Where We Are

Starting at River Valley High School

Kin Vow is starting as a student-run club at River Valley High School. Not a nonprofit. Not an institution. A group of students and a teacher who believe that being heard is a right, not a privilege.

The long-term vision is a chapter model that any school, anywhere, can adopt. Grassroots growth, student-led, from the ground up. But right now, the work is local. Build the space. Prove the concept. Let the students shape what it becomes.
