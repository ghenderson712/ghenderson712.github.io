The Kin Vow

A promise you may choose.

Read it. Consider it. Try the practice. No signature, public declaration, or record of pledges is required.

You can participate before deciding whether to take the vow.

These words describe a practice. They do not give anyone a claim to your trust, forgiveness, closeness, or time.

Open the printable vow
  1. I will treat every person as if their inner world is as vast and valuable as my own.
  2. I will listen to understand, speak honestly, and leave room to be wrong.
  3. I will challenge harm without denying anyone's humanity.
  4. I will respect other people's boundaries and my own.
  5. When I fall short, I will acknowledge what I did and seek repair without demanding forgiveness.
  6. I vow to practice being kin through care for others and promises I can keep.

Keep your convictions.
Respect your boundaries.

You can criticize ideas, institutions, injustice, and false claims. Equal human worth does not make every statement true or every action equally harmful.

You can refuse contact, leave a conversation, or decline a request. Repair may mean changed behavior or respecting distance. It never requires reconciliation.

The deeper framework

Six ideas behind
the practice.

Knowledge, Identity, Narrative, Voice, Oneness, and Worth give KIN VOW its name. You do not need to memorize them to begin.

Knowledge

Stay curious and check what you think you know.

Ask a sincere question. Verify a claim before repeating it.

Identity

Let people describe themselves and choose what to share.

Use the name someone offers. Do not ask them to speak for a whole group.

Narrative

Make room for the experiences behind a person's views.

Ask what shaped a view without demanding a personal story.

Voice

Give people a fair chance to speak, pass, and be heard.

Share the available time. Offer a written response or room to be quiet.

Oneness

Recognize shared humanity while respecting real differences.

Cooperate on something useful without requiring agreement on everything else.

Worth

Treat human dignity as something everyone already has.

Set a boundary without humiliation. Challenge cruelty in your own circle, too.

Give the words a place in your day.
