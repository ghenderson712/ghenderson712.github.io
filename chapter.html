Start a Chapter

Bring Kin Vow to your school.

Any student. Any school. All you need is a few people who believe that being heard is a right, not a privilege.

Why Start a Chapter

Because the gap exists in every school.

Wherever there are students, there are people who know more than they can say. English Learners. Students with learning differences. Introverts. Code-switchers. Kids having a hard day. The gap between inner knowledge and outward expression is universal. Your school is no exception.

A Kin Vow chapter creates a space where that shared struggle becomes the foundation for belonging. Not a remediation program. Not a support group. A community of people who see each other for who they really are, not just what comes out.

What You Need

The basics to get started.

8 to 10 founding members

You do not need a massive group. You need a handful of people who care. Students from any background, any language, any grade level. The only requirement is willingness to show up.

A faculty or staff advisor

Most schools require a full-time staff member to sponsor the club. Find a teacher, counselor, or administrator who believes in the mission. Someone who gets what it means to hold space.

A written constitution

Your school will likely require a club constitution covering the name, purpose, mission, governance structure, and bylaws. We can help you write one that reflects Kin Vow's values.

Officer roles

President, Vice President, Secretary, and Treasurer are standard. Most schools require officers to maintain a 2.0 GPA or higher. The roles are flexible, and the titles can be adapted to fit your culture.

Admin approval

Every school has its own process for approving new clubs. Check with your student activities coordinator or principal. Get the paperwork started early, especially if you want to launch at the start of a semester.

A regular meeting space

A classroom, a library corner, a space that feels safe. Where the group meets matters. Choose somewhere comfortable, accessible, and consistent. The space should feel like it belongs to the members.

The Process

How to launch, step by step.

Phase 1: Groundwork (weeks 1 to 3)

Find your founding members and advisor. Write your constitution. Research your school's club approval process. Start having conversations about what the space will look and feel like. This is where the culture gets set.

Phase 2: Approval (weeks 3 to 5)

Submit your paperwork. Present to whoever needs to approve new clubs. Be ready to explain the mission in plain language: we create safe spaces where every student's voice matters, regardless of how it comes out. Keep it real.

Phase 3: Soft Launch (weeks 5 to 8)

Run your first few meetings with the founding group. Test activities. Build the internal culture before going public. This is where you practice holding space with each other so you can model it for newcomers.

Phase 4: Public Launch (weeks 8 to 10)

Open it up. Recruit. Let people know the space exists. Use word of mouth, morning announcements, posters, social media. The message is simple: you already belong here.

Phase 5: Sustain and Grow (ongoing)

Keep meeting. Keep listening. Let the members shape what the club becomes. The best chapters will look different from each other because they reflect the people in them. That is the whole point.
